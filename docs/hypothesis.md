# Hypothesis

Most executive and director 8-Ks at the 100 largest US companies are filed days after the event. We
believe these filings fall into two kinds:

- **Old news:** the news reached the market before the filing. The stock moved unusually between the event
  and the filing, or the filing says the news was already public.
- **Surprise news:** the filing is the first the market hears of it. The stock was quiet in the gap, and
  the filing gives no sign the news was public.

We think the filing acts as a new, dated headline either way: it draws option demand that raises
implied volatility, and unlike an earnings release, nothing follows that resolves the uncertainty and
brings it back down.

After surprise news, the higher implied volatility pays for a move that is still to come. After old news,
it pays for a move that has already happened. We therefore expect realized volatility to fall short of
implied volatility by more after old-news filings than after surprise filings.
